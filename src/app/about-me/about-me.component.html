About

Building reliable AI for scientific imaging

I am Mazharul Hossain, Ph.D., a Visiting Fellow at the National Cancer Institute, part of the National Institutes of Health. I develop machine-learning and scientific-computing methods for imaging problems where accuracy must hold up beyond the conditions used to build a model. My current work focuses on computational cryo-electron microscopy, while my earlier research explored physics-guided 3D structured-illumination microscopy reconstruction.

During my Ph.D. in Computer Science at the University of Memphis, I developed a body of work in hyperspectral image analysis, including spectral-unmixing, ensemble learning, learned detector selection, supervised and unsupervised anomaly detection, UAV crop segmentation, and cross-domain generalization. This progression culminated in my first-author GMoE-AD paper, published in Sensors in 2026, which combines anomaly detectors, a pretrained hyperspectral model, and a mixture of neural experts to address changes across sensors and datasets.

My master’s work produced a multimodal indoor-mapping solution that combined LiDAR, 360° imagery, and object detection to build public-safety hazard maps. I have also studied explainable AI, with an emphasis on making model requirements, assumptions, and evaluation understandable to the people who use and assess learning systems.
